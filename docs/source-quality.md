## Reading it

**Mean AI focus** near the floor of 30 means a source clears the gate
without ever being really about AI. Volume from such a source is noise
that happens to be admissible.

**In production** is the measure to optimise. It is the difference between
a market-intelligence tool and a clippings file. A source at 0% over a
meaningful number of articles publishes commentary, not deployments —
worth keeping only if nothing else covers its region or segment.

**Use case described** low with production high means a source reports that
things launched without saying what they do. Useful for counting adoption,
weak for understanding it.

## Where to look for more sources

The outlet table above is the shortlist. Beyond it, the pattern in the
data has been consistent:

1. **Trade press beats general press.** A wealth or banking-technology
   title writes about banks deploying things because that is its whole
   beat. A general title writes about AI, and banks appear incidentally.
2. **Search queries beat feed URLs.** Publications retire RSS without
   notice; a site-scoped search reaches them regardless and cannot 404.
3. **Deployment verbs beat topic words.** The query built on "rolls out",
   "goes live" and "deploys" yields fewer articles and better ones than
   the query built on "artificial intelligence".
4. **Regulators and consultancies are low-yield but not worthless.** They
   publish rarely on this topic and matter a great deal when they do.
   Judge them on production evidence, never on volume.
